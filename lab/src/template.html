How Furrow works

1Satellite weather

25 years of daily temperature, rain, soil wetness, sun, wind and humidity for your point from NASA POWER.

2Your field

Slope from elevation data, soil texture from SoilGrids, greenness from NASA MODIS at 250 m.

3Crop calendar

Sowing and harvest dates from your own frost dates and warmth, tuned to USDA usual dates.

4Daily water account

Every day for 25 years: rain fills the soil, the crop drinks, extra drains away.

5Five scores

Water, soil health, soil kept in place, profit and bad years, weighted by your priorities.

6Stress test

300 reshuffles of the years and warmer, drier futures check the answer is not luck.

What it does

  • Real NASA weather for your exact point, 2001 to 2025
  • Crop dates from your own climate, checked against USDA
  • A daily soil-water account for every rotation and year
  • Satellite check of the calendar against your field
  • Your limits, priorities and field size

What it can’t do

  • Weather is a ~50 km grid, not your frost pocket
  • No local prices, tariffs or input costs
  • Soil health is estimated, not measured
  • Pests, weeds and diseases are not modelled
  • Not reviewed by an agronomist yet
You decide. Furrow shows patterns, not instructions. Take an option to an adviser and try it on a strip first.

Words you’ll see

ET (evapotranspiration)

Water leaving the field: evaporation from soil plus water plants breathe out.

Unmet water demand

Share of the water the crop wanted that the soil could not supply.

Soil moisture anomaly

How wet or dry the soil was compared with normal for that month.

Heavy rain

Days with 12.7 mm or more, the storms that move soil.

Growing degree days

Running total of useful warmth that decides when a crop ripens.

NDVI

Satellite greenness score: dense crops high, bare soil low.

Cover crop

Grown between cash crops to protect and feed the soil, not to sell.

Bootstrap

Rebuilding the 25-year record from random years to test if a result is luck.

Do the crop dates match real life?

We compared the sowing and harvest dates Furrow works out from 25 years of NASA temperatures with the "most active" dates in USDA's Usual Planting and Harvesting Dates handbook. We tuned three crop settings until all six dates landed inside USDA's windows, so this shows the model can be made to fit, not that it was right first time.

USDA dates are state-wide and from the 1997 edition. Outside the US we have no published dates to compare with, so the satellite check on the Calendar tab is the only test there.

All the crop numbers we used
Data: NASA POWER daily agroclimatology (T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, PRECTOTCORR, GWETROOT, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, WS2M, RH2M). Field greenness: MODIS MOD13Q1 through the ORNL DAAC subset service. Elevation: Copernicus DEM (ESA) through Open-Meteo. Soil texture: SoilGrids (ISRIC). Live field data: Meteomatics weather API, NASA SMAP L4 soil moisture through AppEEARS, and Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A. Map layers: NASA GIBS (MODIS, SMAP, GPM IMERG). Satellite base map: Esri World Imagery. Crop coefficients follow FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56; reference evapotranspiration uses the FAO Penman-Monteith equation, with Hargreaves as a fallback.
